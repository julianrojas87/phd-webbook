# 5.1 Review of RQ1 and H1

RQ1 was addressed by the works presented in [Chapter 2](ch_2-introduction.md) and [Chapter 4](ch_4_introduction.md). In both chapters, concrete solutions based on Linked Data technologies were described and detailed for supporting the publishing of bicycle parking infrastructure data and the calculation of route compatibility checks of the European railway infrastructure. The requirements and level of technical complexity was high in both scenarios, considering the number and heterogeneous nature of the data that needed to be modelled and integrated to support the respective use cases. For instance, the static and live data types present in Velopark or the different original relational data models, plus the externally provided data sources in the case of ERA. In both cases, creation (manually via Web forms in Velopark and; declaratively via RML rules in ERA), publishing (a catalog and a collection of thousands of decentralised data resources in Velopark and; a 35+M triples knowledge graph) and querying (for discovery and visualisation purposes in Velopark and; for geospatial fragmentation and route compatibility assessments in ERA) of RDF data was required. Both solutions have shown remarkable signs of adoption, having the Velopark platform being used by over 70 municipality public authorities in Belgium and also having ERA making the official decision of adopting Linked Data technologies as the default technology stack for any data related endeavours, added to the inclusion of the ERA vocabulary as a building block and reference ontology in the recent EU Commission communications regarding the Common European Data Spaces.

Based on this evidence, I move onto accepting H1 and stating that Linked Data technologies are mature enough for building complex and semantically interoperable data management systems in production-ready settings.
