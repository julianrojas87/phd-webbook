# 1.6 Impact

Measuring impact is a rather subjective task, but perhaps the main type of impact that can be highlighted and that succinctly summarises the main achievements and outcomes of this PhD dissertation is: *Adoption*. Thanks to having the opportunity of executing the different works that comprises this PhD in real environments and together with real organisations that have an active role in the EU transport domain, it is possible to see that some of the products resulting from this PhD work have been and continue to be used, maintained and evolved. Next, a brief and contextualised description is given around the most important and adopted results from this PhD:

* **Velopark:** Velopark was a project funded by the Federal Ministry of Mobility and Transport in Belgium, with the objective of creating an interoperable platform to openly publish bicycle parking facilities information. During the project an ontology was created following Semantic Web standards and involving an important number of stakeholders, namely the [Open Velopark vocabulary](https://data.velopark.be/openvelopark/vocabulary). This ontology was used to guide the implementation of a [Web application](https://www.velopark.be/en/pro) oriented towards public authorities and private bicycle parking managers (BPMs) that allowed them to (decentrally) manage, describe and publish the data about their facilities. Furthermore, an independent [user-oriented Web application](https://www.velopark.be/en) was developed to visualise the data over an interactive map interface. By the end of the project in 12/2019 the Velopark platform published a total of 1599 facilities with an active participation of 31 Belgian municipalities and 4 BPMs. At the time of writing (05/2024) the total number of published facilities is 3017, with an active participation of 71 Belgian municipalities and 11 BPMs. This shows an important increase and continued use of this set of tools, all built based on Linked Data technologies.

* **ERA Vocabulary and Knowledge Graph:** Similarly, I have been involved since 2020 in a direct collaboration with the European Agency for Railways (ERA). The collaboration started with an interoperability challenge in mind, where at least 2 of the base registries managed by the agency needed to establish an efficient interoperability mechanism that allowed them to support route compatibility checks (RCC) over the EU railway infrastructure. A semantic interoperability effort based on W3C Semantic standards, started with the creation of the [ERA Vocabulary](https://data-interop.era.europa.eu/era-vocabulary/). Later a whole system architecture was designed and implemented using open source tools, that allowed the creation and publishing of the [ERA Knowledge Graph](https://www.era.europa.eu/domains/registers/era-knowledge-graph_en). Lastly, a modern [user-oriented Web application](https://data-interop.era.europa.eu/route-compatibility) was developed to support the RCC use case. The successful outcome of these efforts resulted in the [official decision](https://www.era.europa.eu/content/decision-n%C2%B0250-management-board-european-union-agency-railways-adopting-roadmap-data-and_en) of the agency to make Linked Data technologies the default setting for any future data-related endeavours. More importantly, given ERA's position of an authority for cross-border rail traffic in Europe, the ERA Vocabulary has been made a building block and reference ontology in the recent EU Commission communications regarding the [Common European Data Spaces](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-11/COM_2023_751.pdf) and in the EU Implementing Regulation 2023/1694 [@@EUC_2023_1694]. Additionally, the related publication (detailed in Chapter 4) was awarded the Best Paper Award for the In-Use track in the [ISWC 2022 conference](https://twitter.com/iswc_conf/status/1453752339475701766).
